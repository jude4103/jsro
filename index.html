Mission

Making life multiplanetary

It all started with an obsession for rockets and space. In 2014, a ten-year-old who knew of exactly one space agency decided the world needed another, gave it his name, and drew its first logo by hand.

Since 2014, JSRO has had one aim: to help make humans a multiplanetary species. Today that means following every launch, getting people outside for every sky event worth seeing, and building a crew who care about getting there as much as we do.

Issue 001November 2026

Hello from JSRO

If you're reading this, you've signed up for something I started when I was ten. Back then JSRO was a name in a notebook and a logo drawn with a sketch pen. It took twelve years, and for the first time, people other than me.

These dispatches will be short. I'll write when there's something worth your attention: a night worth staying up for, a launch worth watching live, and now and then some news from the agency.

Two things for the next few weeks. ISRO is aiming to fly Gaganyaan G1 in December — the first uncrewed test of India's crew capsule, with the robot Vyommitra riding along. And on the night of 13–14 December the Geminids peak under a thin Moon. Find somewhere dark, lie back, and give it twenty minutes.

Thanks for being here at the start.

— Jude
